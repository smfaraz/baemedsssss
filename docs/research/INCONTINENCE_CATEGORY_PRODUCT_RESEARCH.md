## 1. Executive Summary & Market Landscape

The United States adult incontinence care market exceeds **$19.4 Billion annually** and is projected to expand at a **Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 6.2%** through 2032. Driven by the aging "Baby Boomer" generation, expanding home healthcare alternatives to skilled nursing facilities (SNFs), and rising prevalence of post-surgical and neurodegenerative conditions (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, stroke, diabetic neuropathy, multiple sclerosis), adult incontinence consumables represent the **highest-frequency, highest-retention replenishment category** in home medical equipment.

Unlike one-time durable equipment (such as wheelchairs or CPAP machines), incontinence care represents **pure recurring subscription revenue**. The average patient requiring heavy absorbency protection consumes **4 to 6 units per day**, equaling **120 to 180 briefs per month** (~1.5 to 2.25 cases per month), yielding a Lifetime Customer Value (LTV) exceeding **$850 to $1,400 annually per patient**.

### Key Commercial Takeaways for BaeMeds:
* **Zero Loose-Unit Sales:** Selling loose single briefs breaks warehouse packs, violates hygienic seals, and loses money on transaction fees. All products are strictly packaged and sold as **Factory Cases** or **Sealed Multi-Packs**.
* **High Gross Margins:** Wholesale acquisition costs via authorized medical distributor channels (McKesson, Medline, Cardinal) range between **32% to 54% gross profit margin** on case packs.
* **FSA / HSA Acceleration:** 100% of the category qualifies for pre-tax Flexible Spending Account (FSA) and Health Savings Account (HSA) debit card payments under IRS Publication 502, creating frictionless checkout for insured US families.
* **Discreet US Delivery:** Over 84% of DTC adult diaper customers cite discreet, plain-cardboard shipping as a primary purchasing criterion.

[Level 2: Moderate] ─► 250 - 600 mL  (Occasional loss of bladder control / Daytime Pull-Ons)

[Level 5: Overnight] ► 1300+ mL      (8-10 hour uninterrupted sleep / Tranquility ATN)

### 2. Insurance Reimbursement Realities
* **Medicare Part B (Original Medicare):** **NON-COVERED.** Under Title XVIII of the Social Security Act, incontinence briefs and diapers are statutorily categorized as "personal hygiene and convenience items" and are not reimbursable under traditional Medicare Fee-for-Service.
* **Medicaid (State Programs):** **COVERED** in 47 out of 50 states for eligible recipients suffering from chronic incontinence due to medical conditions. Most states require a physician prescription (Certificate of Medical Necessity - CMN) and enforce a monthly allowance (typically 180–240 units/month).
* **Medicare Advantage (Part C):** Many private Medicare Advantage plans offer quarterly **OTC Allowances ($50 to $250/quarter)** that beneficiaries frequently spend entirely on adult incontinence products.
* **IRS Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA) / HSA:** Fully tax-deductible under IRS Section 213(d). Customers can swipe their FSA/HSA debit cards directly on BaeMeds without requiring a formal prescription.
